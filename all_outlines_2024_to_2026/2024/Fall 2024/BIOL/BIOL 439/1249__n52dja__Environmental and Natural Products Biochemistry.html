Course Description

Calendar Description for BIOL 439

This course deals with the functions, distribution, and environmental ramifications of natural compounds produced by plants and other biological systems. Natural products are those compounds usually described as secondary metabolites, i.e., those apparently non-essential products whose physiological and ecological functions are either obscure or are of peripheral importance to the organism. However, many of these non-essential products have profound competitive, economic, and pharmacological significance; and as research proceeds, their physiological roles within the parent organisms are becoming clearer. As well, this course has a strong emphasis on how environmental chemical and physical processes impact on living organisms and their biochemistry.

View requirements for BIOL 439

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course students should be able to:
Identify different types and classes of plant secondary metabolites.
Describe the importance of physical, biological, and environmental concepts related to plant secondary metabolites.
Discuss opportunities and challenges for using plant secondary metabolites.
Integrate physical, biological, and environmental concepts related to different plant secondary metabolites.

Tentative Course Schedule

Topics 

  • Introduction: Products of primary and secondary metabolism. Importance of secondary metabolites. Review of isomerism.
  • Isoprenoids: Mevalonate pathway. Terpenes, carotenoids, steroids, and polyisoprenes. 
  • Allelopathic Chemicals: Compounds that provide a competitive advantage to an organism.
  • Phenolics: The shikimic acid pathway with an emphasis on the flavonoids.
  • Plant-derived pharmaceutical compounds
    • E.g., Taxol: An important pharmaceutical chemical produced by a plant.
  • Environmental Photochemistry and Photobiology: Environmental photobiology, phototoxic compounds, photodynamic therapy, and photochemistry of beer

1) Two required individual reflections at mid and end of term. Focus on lectures and class discussions for the 6-week period.

  • Dates and deadlines for individual reflections and quizzes are indicated on LEARN; times are based on Eastern Time (ET) i.e., Waterloo, Ontario time. 
  • Individual reflections: Prepare two separate 2-3 pages reflections that is based on topics, discussions and lectures covered in the first and last 6-week periods of the course i.e., you will submit a total of two reflections. Choice of topic does NOT need my approval. NO MORE THAN 3 PAGES. The 2-3 pages are for the body of the text. Title Page, figures, tables, and bibliography are extra pages. You must include references from traditional, peer reviewed literature (i.e., a published book or refereed journal). All pages must be numbered.

  • Submit the written individual reflections on due dates stipulated on LEARN. Do not email assignments. Include your name and student number in your reflections. Late submission of the individual reflections will result to ZERO mark for the assignment.

If you do not know how to do this, consult the University of Waterloo Library Citation/ Style Guide, and contact me if you need additional assistance. Administrative Policy

Generative AI

This course includes the independent development and practice of specific skills, such as discussion of new knowledge in the field of natural products and their competitive, economic, and pharmacological significance. Therefore, the use of Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) trained using large language models (LLM) or other methods to produce text, images, music, or code, like Chat GPT, DALL-E, or GitHub CoPilot, is not permitted in this class. Unauthorized use in this course, such as running course materials through GenAI or using GenAI to complete a course assessment is considered a violation of Policy 71 (plagiarism or unauthorized aids or assistance). Work produced with the assistance of AI tools does not represent the author’s original work and is therefore in violation of the fundamental values of academic integrity including honesty, trust, respect, fairness, responsibility and courage (ICAI, n.d.). 

You should be prepared to show your work. To demonstrate your learning, you should keep your rough notes, including research notes, brainstorming, and drafting notes. You may be asked to submit these notes along with earlier drafts of their work, either through saved drafts or saved versions of a document. If the use of GenAI is suspected where not permitted, you may be asked to meet with your instructor or TA to provide explanations to support the submitted material as being your original work. Through this process, if you have not sufficiently supported your work, academic misconduct allegations may be brought to the Associate Dean. 

In addition, you should be aware that the legal/copyright status of generative AI inputs and outputs is unclear. More information is available from the Copyright Advisory Committee:  https://uwaterloo.ca/copyright...
