Course Description

Calendar Description for GBDA 301

In this course, students will create critical design projects that explore the intersection of design, technology, and culture in a global context. Students will experiment with and prototype objects/systems that engage with contemporary ideas and technologies, while reflecting on how these objects/systems shape our everyday experiences. Students will be guided through one or more design cycles that unite design thinking with critical reflection through the practical application of theory. [Note: Offered at Stratford campus.]

Prereq: GBDA 202; Level at least 3A Global Business and Digital Arts students

Term Project Brief

Over the past five years, the number of post-secondary students facing mental health challenges has risen significantly, with international students experiencing even higher rates. To address this concern, you will design a speculative extended reality (XR) experience that fosters connections between international and domestic students at the University of Waterloo through positive coping strategies. 

A positive coping strategy for mental health is creating a positive and powerful message that motivates individuals to take charge of their mental well-being. This can encompass emphasizing relaxation, reducing the negative perception of mental health issues, fostering a sense of control and self-care, implementing practical measures to enhance one's overall health and manage stress, and ultimately employing positive and inclusive language. Positive coping strategies include relaxation techniques, physical activity, sleep, social connection, expressing yourself creatively, and even spending more time outside in the natural world. You can choose any positive coping strategy for this project. Please note that you are not to design systems that diagnose or give medical advice to students but rather focus on encouraging healthy habits for student well-being. 

Term Project Goals

  • Utilize interactive elements to create a captivating XR experience.
  • Offer valuable insights into the challenges faced by both domestic and international students regarding mental health.
  • Raise awareness, promote understanding, and encourage open discussions about mental health issues among undergraduate students at the University of Waterloo.

Term Project Requirements

  • XR Platforms: develop and iterate a speculative XR prototype
  • Deliverables: This term project is divided into three deliverables: an individual preliminary visual report, a group low-fidelity prototype presentation, and a high-fidelity prototype presentation. You must implement your design skills to develop a prototype using a wizard-of-oz method. We will not build a functional prototype in this class or use headsets during the semester.
  • Mental Health Topic: Explore a mental health topic relevant to the student experience (consider the list in Week 1’s slides or propose your own with justification).
  • Focus on Empathy and Understanding: Strive to create an experience that builds empathy and understanding between international and domestic students.
  • Interactive Elements: Incorporate interactive elements that engage users and enhance the learning experience.

Creating a Safe Space

We understand that mental health is a sensitive topic. We encourage open and respectful classroom dialogue while fostering a safe space for exploration and feedback. Resources will be provided to support students encountering challenging emotions during the project. 

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course students should be able to:
Demonstrate a comprehensive understanding primary and secondary research and use that knowledge to inform the design of an XR Prototype.
Understand the core principles of speculative design including critical thinking, fiction prototyping, user engagement, and social commentary and discuss challenges and opportunities.
Manage a semester-long project by effectively scoping the project and creating actionable goals for each project deadline.
Developing a strategy to facilitate positive and productive team communication
Analyze the role of user research in designing XR experiences.

Generative AI

Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) trained using large language models (LLM) or other methods to produce text, images, music, or code, like Chat GPT, DALL-E, or GitHub CoPilot, may be used for assignments in this class with proper documentation, citation, and acknowledgement. Secondly, all AI use must be in English, as we cannot confirm that it was used appropriately in other languages. However, you can use it with proper documentation to help fix spelling and grammatical errors. The work produced with the uncredited assistance of AI tools does not represent the author’s original work. 

Accordingly, you MUST cite any GenAI tools using the documentation supplied in Week 1. Uncredited use of generative AI, such as running course materials through GenAI, translating text from another language into English, or using GenAI to complete a course assessment, violates Policy 71 (plagiarism or unauthorized aids or assistance). 

Recommendations for how to cite GenAI in student work at the University of Waterloo may be found in the Library: https://subjectguides.uwaterlo.... Please be aware that generative AI is known to falsify references to other work and may fabricate facts and inaccurately express ideas. GenAI generates content based on the input of other human authors and may, therefore, contain inaccuracies or reflect biases. 

In addition, you should be aware that the legal/copyright status of generative AI inputs and outputs is unclear. Exercise caution when using large portions of content from AI sources, especially images. More information is available from the Copyright Advisory Committee:  https://uwaterloo.ca/copyright.... 

All the work you turn in for this class, including any that uses generative AI, is your responsibility. If using AI for any element of your projects, you must (1) state that you used Gen AI and (2) document the process using the blank template available on Learn and submit it with your assignments. Undocumented use of GenAI for your assignments or presentations is otherwise considered a violation of Policy 71. 

Working in Teams

Individual and group assignments are indicated in the syllabus. Individual assignments are to be completed by each student alone and in collaboration with other students as indicated (i.e. User testing, in-class brainstorming sessions, etc.). Collaboration on producing individual deliverables (i.e. A1) constitutes unauthorized collaboration as outlined in Policy 71.

Group assignments must be completed collaboratively, including effectively managing goals, tasks, and workflow, completing deliverables on time and schedule, managing change and learning from mistakes, and engaging in healthy conflict. Each team member is expected to make tangible contributions to the project, drawing from their expertise and/or developing new skills and expertise. These contributions will be assessed in an evaluation for each group assignment; a written contribution response is required in the appendix of each group assignment. More details on this will be available in the individual assignment outlines. 

For the group assignments, each team member will receive the same “basis” for their grade on the report. At the same time, adjustments can be made for individual contributions or lack thereof, at the instructor's discretion, based on the feedback from the peer review quiz. This means that you must make a direct contribution to the development of the project and make your team work effectively.

Also, during the semester, as soon as you perceive that a member consistently does not make a fair contribution to the team, you are responsible for communicating this to the instructor to resolve such issues as soon as possible and not wait until late in the term or after a deliverable has been submitted. When you contact the instructor, please include any additional work you had to complete on behalf of your group member, along with proof that you at least attempted to reach out to your group member and waited a reasonable amount of time for a response. 

When it is determined by the instructor that none of the team members have been known to have reached out to the no-contributing student to engage them to contribute more, then the guidelines in this policy would not apply, and there would be no associated penalties as per the instructor's discretion. 

When there is deemed a lack of contribution from one or more of the group members, the grade of the deliverable does not increase for the other team members who did contribute to the final submission. The deliverable is graded initially without considering the lack of contribution to have a baseline grade that the deliverable has earned. At this point, the quality of the deliverable does not increase because of the lack of contribution issues. This means the baseline grade is the highest grade the deliverable has earned, and therefore, that is the highest grade that can be offered to a student on the team that submitted the deliverable. 

The following penalty guidelines will be used to examine and categorize each lack of contribution case:

No penalty: To the best of their ability, the group members meet the deadlines and expectations agreed upon in the group charter. 

10% penalty*: One or more group members have attempted to reach out to the student and have not engaged or communicated with the group within a reasonable amount of time. The work submitted by the student was given to the group in a reasonable amount of time, but it was not as the group deemed acceptable and needed to be redone mostly. 

25% penalty*: If the student contributed at least half of their expected work, but not all content, in a reasonable amount of time, it was not as the group deemed acceptable and needed to be mostly re-done. This assumes one or more group members have attempted to reach out to the student to engage. 

50% penalty*: If the student contributed less than half of their expected work in a reasonable amount of time, it was not as the group deemed acceptable and needed to be mostly re-done. This assumes one or more group members have attempted to reach out to the student to engage. 

100% penalty*: If the student did not contribute any work of their own in a reasonable amount of time and/or if the work was stolen, bought or used from a different course. If a student did not contribute any work, this is considered an academic offence, and an investigation into the project will be conducted.  

* % penalty applied to the graded portion of the deliverable. For example, if a 10% penalty was applied and the student earned 60 points out of 100 and the 10% penalty was applied, the student would have a 6-point reduction based on the 10% of the 60 points earned.

Academic Offences

If a student is suspected of engaging in unauthorized collaboration, plagiarism, unauthorized aid or assistance, misrepresentation, copying of one's work from another class assessment or theft of intellectual property, they will be reported by the instructor to the Associate Dean of Arts.

When grades are released, the student will receive a comment on the ungraded assignment stating concerns about an academic offence and that they are being investigated. No other information will be able to be released to the student until the investigation is complete.

For group work, you must identify the portions of the assignment you were responsible for on the peer review quiz. If there are concerns about plagiarism and identification is absent, all students in the group will be held responsible for violations of academic integrity in the group submission. 

Once the investigation is complete, if no academic offence is found, the student or group will receive their grade as normal. However, if there is an academic office, the student or group will receive an email from the instructor or the Associate Dean of Arts office indicating concerns about plagiarism. 

The severity of the penalty for plagiarism depends on the quantity of plagiarized content and the extent to which the student failed to attribute the plagiarized materials. The impact of the penalty also depends on the value of the submission. Extenuating circumstances are sometimes considered case-by-case, and sometimes less severe penalties are considered. Repeat offenders will receive more severe penalties. 

If your assignment is flagged for plagiarism, you have two choices: accept the allegation and penalty. Then, you will receive an official resolution letter from the Associate Dean. You can request a formal investigation to dispute the allegation and penalty. If so, you will meet directly with the Associate Dean to discuss your objections and relevant evidence. 

Read the University of Waterloo Policy 70 (Student petitions and grievances), Policy 71 (Student discipline), and Policy 72 (Student appeals), and they will provide you with information regarding the procedures that will take place.

Academic Freedom at the University of Waterloo

Academic freedom carries the duty to use that freedom consistent with the scholarly obligation to base teaching and research on an honest and ethical quest for knowledge. In the context of this policy, 'academic freedom' refers to academic activities, including teaching and scholarship, as articulated in the principles set out in the Memorandum of Agreement between the FAUW and the University of Waterloo, 1998 (Article 6). A pronoun is a word used to describe you when spoken of in the third person (i.e., when not being directly addressed), such as they, she, he, or xie. For more information, visit the pronouns FAQ resources page.  Effective spring 2023, as a current student, you can choose to provide your pronouns in Quest and change or delete your selection at any time.  

Should you choose to include your pronouns in Quest, your pronouns will be accessible by instructors, advisors, and employees with access to Quest. If no pronoun is added, the field in Quest will remain blank.  The University is working to provide your pronouns to other systems across campus (e.g., LEARN, WaterlooWorks) to support using your pronouns across all platforms.  

For detailed instructions on making or updating your pronoun selection in Quest, visit How do I view or update my personal information. As there is no mechanism in Quest to automatically notify instructors of updates in pronoun information, please let your instructor know if you have updated this information.

Intellectual Property  

Students should be aware that this course contains the intellectual property of their instructor, TA, and/or the University of Waterloo. Intellectual property includes items such as:  

  • Lecture content, spoken and written (and any audio/video recording thereof);
  • Lecture handouts, presentations, and other materials prepared for the course (e.g., Slides);
  • Questions or solution sets from various types of assessments (e.g., assignments, quizzes, tests, final exams) and
  • Work protected by copyright (e.g., any work authored by the instructor or TA or used by the instructor or TA with permission of the copyright owner).

Course materials and the intellectual property contained therein are used to enhance a student’s educational experience. However, sharing this intellectual property without the owner’s permission violates intellectual property rights.  For this reason, it is necessary to ask the instructor, TA, and/or the University of Waterloo for permission before uploading and sharing the intellectual property of others online (e.g., in an online repository).  

Permission from an instructor, TA or the University is also necessary before sharing the intellectual property of others from completed courses with students taking the same/similar courses in subsequent terms/years.  In many cases, instructors might be happy to allow distribution of certain materials. However, doing so without expressed permission is considered a violation of intellectual property rights.  

Please alert the instructor if you become aware of intellectual property belonging to others (past or present) circulating through the student body or online. The intellectual property rights owner should know (and may have already consented).

University Policy

Academic integrity: In order to maintain a culture of academic integrity, members of the University of Waterloo community are expected to promote honesty, trust, fairness, respect and responsibility. [Check the Office of Academic Integrity for more information.]

Grievance: A student who believes that a decision affecting some aspect of their university life has been unfair or unreasonable may have grounds for initiating a grievance. Read Policy 70, Student Petitions and Grievances, Section 4. When in doubt, please be certain to contact the department’s administrative assistant who will provide further assistance.

Discipline: A student is expected to know what constitutes academic integrity to avoid committing an academic offence, and to take responsibility for their actions. [Check the Office of Academic Integrity for more information.] A student who is unsure whether an action constitutes an offence, or who needs help in learning how to avoid offences (e.g., plagiarism, cheating) or about “rules” for group work/collaboration should seek guidance from the course instructor, academic advisor, or the undergraduate associate dean. For information on categories of offences and types of penalties, students should refer to Policy 71, Student Discipline. For typical penalties, check Guidelines for the Assessment of Penalties.

Appeals: A decision made or penalty imposed under Policy 70, Student Petitions and Grievances (other than a petition) or Policy 71, Student Discipline may be appealed if there is a ground. A student who believes they have a ground for an appeal should refer to Policy 72, Student Appeals.

Note for students with disabilities: AccessAbility Services, located in Needles Hall, Room 1401, collaborates with all academic departments to arrange appropriate accommodations for students with disabilities without compromising the academic integrity of the curriculum. If you require academic accommodations to lessen the impact of your disability, please register with AccessAbility Services at the beginning of each academic term.

Turnitin.com: Text matching software (Turnitin®) may be used to screen assignments in this course. Turnitin® is used to verify that all materials and sources in assignments are documented. Students' submissions are stored on a U.S. server, therefore students must be given an alternative (e.g., scaffolded assignment or annotated bibliography), if they are concerned about their privacy and/or security. Students will be given due notice, in the first week of the term and/or at the time assignment details are provided, about arrangements and alternatives for the use of Turnitin in this course.

It is the responsibility of the student to notify the instructor if they, in the first week of term or at the time assignment details are provided, wish to submit alternate assignment.
