Course Description

Calendar Description for CS 492

This course is designed to consider the problems encountered by individuals, organizations and society as computer technology is adopted, with a view towards assessing possible courses of action.

Prereq: CS 240/240E; Computer Science students only

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course students should be able to:
Communicate effectively in an oral and written matter about societal issues related with computing

This page contains elaboration on the structure of the CS492 offering in Spring 2024, including sections on lectures, the marking scheme, the late policy, assignments, and role-playing exercises. Information on University policies (such as academic integrity) can be found here.

Lectures

Lectures are scheduled for two eighty-minute slots per week (TR 8:30-10:00am (001), TR 11:30-12:50pm (002)). The class will be run more like a discussion seminar than a standard lecture. Students are expected to do the preparatory readings (as listed in the timetable) in advance; discussion will commence using those readings as a base. The instructor may communicate for five minutes here and there in order to set the stage, make a technical point, summarize, or concentrate attention. Student participation is important not only to facilitate the learning process but because marks are assessed for class participation.

Marking scheme

Final marks are based on: 

  • 50% assignments (5 assignments equally weighted)
  • 25% role playing exercise
  • 25% class participation, based on
    • attending classes
    • discussion of the topic of the day
    • participation in discussion during RPEs
    • discussion on newsgroup, including In The News (up to 1:00pm on Tuesday July 30th - see below for more information)
    • RPE form for the 8:30am class (001)
    • RPE form for the 11:30am class (002)
    • effectiveness in communication and thought
    • preparation and depth of treatment of the topic
    • content: how realistic/convincing the position is and how clearly it has been articulated
    • how much progress has been made jointly and
    • how well each team has succeeded in making the joint communique address their concerns

Assignment Screening

Text matching software (Turnitin) will be used to screen assignments in this course. This is being done to verify that use of all material and sources in assignments is documented. In the first week of the term, details will be provided about the arrangements for the use of Turnitin and alternatives in this course. See Administrative Policy below for more information and links. ChatGPT and LLMs: Using ChatGPT (or similar tools such as GitHub Copilot, Grammarly, Quillbot, WordAi, Wordtune, Jasper, AHelp Paraphraser, PrePosteSEO, ArticleRewriter, Rewriter, SciSpace, etc. that use articificial intelligence or any third party to generate text, code, or visual images) for content generation and submitting it as one’s own original work is a violation of the University of Waterloo’s Policy 71 (Student Discipline).
