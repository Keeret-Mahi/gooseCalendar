Lectures

I (Jennifer) will be teaching the vast majority of the lectures this term. A TA, Sahar or Salman, may teach a lecture or two if I am sick or otherwise unavailable. A few lectures will include guest speakers from University of Waterloo support units which may assist you with your course work. Our lectures are bright and early at 8:30AM (just when everyone wants to learn math!) Monday, Wednesday, and Friday mornings in E7-3343 with a few exceptions (see 'Make-up Lectures and Exceptions' below).

The course is divided into 10 topics. Before the first lecture in each topic, I will post fill-in-the-blank slides in PDF form on LEARN (Content > Topic # > Material). During lectures, these slides will be projected onto the lecture hall screen. Blanks will be filled in class using a combination of uncovering short, typed answers, and using the podium tablet and pen to hand-write answers. Once a topic is complete, I will post complete slides with typed answers in PDF form on LEARN. Topics will typically cover: 1) some background and context, 2) the expected solution process, and 3) increasingly complex example problems and solutions (up to and including exam-level problems). During lectures, I will sometimes allow time for everyone to attempt an example problem before we take up the answer as a class (requesting sequential solution steps from the class). This is a learning process so uncertain/ incorrect student answers are expected when taking up solutions in class. Questions can be asked at any point during lectures by raising your hand. I may also be able to answer questions before/ after class if time permits (though we may need to step into the hall to allow the next instructor to set-up).

To aid communication, I will wear a microphone and may have live, auto-generated captions visible on the screen in each lecture. Lectures will be recorded* so you can view them later if needed/ desired (see ME203 Notice of Recording on LEARN). If you must enter the class late or leave early (e.g., for an interview), please try to do so quietly to minimize disruption for your classmates. Masks are welcome in the classroom. I will often wear a mask in class - either because I feel I need to (e.g., I will be in close contact with individuals at higher risk from illness) or because I am showing support for those who must wear a mask in public. Accordingly, I request that you not come to class if you are sick - we have recordings so you can watch from home and/ or catch up when you are feeling better.

Course Description

Calendar Description for ME 203

Solution of ordinary differential equations. First and higher order differential equations. Nonlinear equations. Linear equations with constant and variable coefficients. Systems of linear equations. Applications involving simple dynamical systems and principles of mass, momentum and heat conservation will emphasize the role of ordinary differential equations in understanding the behaviour of physical systems. Introduction to the Laplace transform method for solving ordinary differential equations. [Offered: F, S]

Prereq: ME 201, Level at least 2B Mechanical Engineering

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course students should be able to:
<b>classify</b> Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) [1a]
<b>use</b> appropriate analytical method(s) to solve different types of ODE problems [1a, 5c]
<b>formulate and justify</b> mathematical models for the behaviour of simplified engineering systems (i.e., set up ODEs, boundary conditions to model a system) [2b, 3b, 3c, 7b, 12b]
<b>devise and employ</b> a process for selecting appropriate analytical method(s) to solve engineering ODEs [1a, 5a, 12b, 12c]
<b>apply</b> engineering principles when solving simplified systems and ODEs [2b, 3b, 3c, 6a, 6b, 7b, 7c, 12a,12b]

Project

The course project relates to the simulation of a system using the methods that you will learn in the course this term. For the project, you will work in groups of three. You must self-enroll in a group by June 3 through LEARN (Connect > Groups > Project). Each group will select one of three challenge problems to solve. The expectation is that you work together with your group, each contributing approximately equal effort to the finished project and report. Detailed project instructions will be provided mid- to late-May. The initial project submission is a short (typically 2-3 page) draft introduction for the report plus a group charter (2-3 pages). We anticipate that each student might require up to 5 hours for the initial submission. The final project report will be a formal report (typically 15-20 pages plus hand-written work in the appendices). We expect that each student might dedicate up to 20 hours to the final project (in addition to the initial submission).  Rubrics for both project submissions are posted on LEARN (Resources > Rubrics). The initial project LEARN Dropbox submission deadline is June 10, 2024 at 4:30 PM. The due date for the final project report is July 29, 2024 at 4:30 PM.

Note: The project challenge instructions start quite detailed and become progressively vague (intentionally). The "answers" to the prompts become more judgement-based and different groups can end up going down different, though not necessarily less valid, paths. As this is likely a new experience for many, we are expecting requests for group meetings to answer student questions. Please contact Salman or Sahar (depending on which challenge you select) if you have questions or get stuck as you proceed through the project. There are also supports available from the Library, Writing and Communication Centre, and Student Success Office.

Academic Integrity: You are expected to complete the initial submission in pairs without discussing with other classmates. For the final project, you may consult with other groups, and include a list of students you consulted in the background work portion of the final project submission. All preliminary calculations and report writing must be completed within your group. Use of generative AI is permitted IFF (if and only if) you reference the AI according to the guidelines in the statement (below), using the library guide (link below) AND you clearly list how, and in what section(s), the AI was used. Extreme caution is advised.

Late Penalty: 10% of the value of the submission will be deducted per day late (including weekend days). The first late day will start June 11 8:00 AM and July 30 8:00 AM for the initial and final submissions, respectively. Marking and feedback may be delayed.

Rescheduling Co-op Interviews: Follow the co-op process for rescheduling co-op interviews for conflicts to graded assignments (e.g., midterms, tests, and final exams). Attendance at co-operative work-term employment interviews is not considered to be a valid reason to miss a test.

Generative AI

Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) trained using large language models (LLM) or other methods to produce text, images, music, or code, like Chat GPT, DALL-E, or GitHub CoPilot, may be used in this course with proper documentation, citation, and acknowledgement. Permitted uses of and expectations for using GenAI will discussed in class and outlined on assignment instructions. 

Recommendations for how to cite generative AI in student work at the University of Waterloo may be found through the Library:  https://subjectguides.uwaterlo.... Please be aware that generative AI is known to falsify references to other work and may fabricate facts and inaccurately express ideas. GenAI generates content based on the input of other human authors and may therefore contain inaccuracies or reflect biases. 

In addition, you should be aware that the legal/copyright status of generative AI inputs and outputs is unclear. Exercise caution when using large portions of content from AI sources, especially images. More information is available from the Copyright Advisory Committee:  https://uwaterloo.ca/copyright... 

You are accountable for the content and accuracy of all work you submit in this class, including any supported by generative AI. 

  • Details on when GenAI can and cannot be used in this course are detailed above by assessment. More detail will be provided related to the project when the project is introduced.

University Policy

Academic integrity: In order to maintain a culture of academic integrity, members of the University of Waterloo community are expected to promote honesty, trust, fairness, respect and responsibility. [Check the Office of Academic Integrity for more information.]

Grievance: A student who believes that a decision affecting some aspect of their university life has been unfair or unreasonable may have grounds for initiating a grievance. Read Policy 70, Student Petitions and Grievances, Section 4. When in doubt, please be certain to contact the department’s administrative assistant who will provide further assistance.

Discipline: A student is expected to know what constitutes academic integrity to avoid committing an academic offence, and to take responsibility for their actions. [Check the Office of Academic Integrity for more information.] A student who is unsure whether an action constitutes an offence, or who needs help in learning how to avoid offences (e.g., plagiarism, cheating) or about “rules” for group work/collaboration should seek guidance from the course instructor, academic advisor, or the undergraduate associate dean. For information on categories of offences and types of penalties, students should refer to Policy 71, Student Discipline. For typical penalties, check Guidelines for the Assessment of Penalties.

Appeals: A decision made or penalty imposed under Policy 70, Student Petitions and Grievances (other than a petition) or Policy 71, Student Discipline may be appealed if there is a ground. A student who believes they have a ground for an appeal should refer to Policy 72, Student Appeals.

Note for students with disabilities: AccessAbility Services, located in Needles Hall, Room 1401, collaborates with all academic departments to arrange appropriate accommodations for students with disabilities without compromising the academic integrity of the curriculum. If you require academic accommodations to lessen the impact of your disability, please register with AccessAbility Services at the beginning of each academic term.

Turnitin.com: Text matching software (Turnitin®) may be used to screen assignments in this course. Turnitin® is used to verify that all materials and sources in assignments are documented. Students' submissions are stored on a U.S. server, therefore students must be given an alternative (e.g., scaffolded assignment or annotated bibliography), if they are concerned about their privacy and/or security. Students will be given due notice, in the first week of the term and/or at the time assignment details are provided, about arrangements and alternatives for the use of Turnitin in this course.

It is the responsibility of the student to notify the instructor if they, in the first week of term or at the time assignment details are provided, wish to submit alternate assignment.
