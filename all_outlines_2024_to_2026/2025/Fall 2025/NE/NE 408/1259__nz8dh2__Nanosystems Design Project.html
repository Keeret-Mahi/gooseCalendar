Course Description

Calendar Description for NE 408

Design work for the project proposed in NE307, culminating in a progress report presentation.

View requirements for NE 408

The Fourth Year Design Project is a three course sequence with NE307 “Introduction to Nanosystems Design” in the 3B term, NE408 “Nanosystems Design Project” in the 4A term, and NE409 “Nanosystems Design Project and Symposium” in the 4B term.

In NE408 teams will perform design work for the projects proposed in NE307. Teams will use engineering design analysis, modelling and/or simulations for the purpose of refining and validating their design conceptually in preparation for the design prototyping stage. They also start the prototyping stage, to be completed in NE409, in which they build and test a physical prototype of their design for the purpose of demonstrating that the design successfully meets the desired specifications (thus meets customer requirements).

By the end of NE409, teams are expected to deliver a design prototype that is functionally feasible, meeting engineering specifications that your team developed, based on the project requirements and constraints.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course students should be able to...
1. Identify, formulate, analyze, and solve complex engineering problems utilizing university level mathematics and applied engineering fundamentals. (KB and PA)
2. Investigate complex open ended engineering problems using appropriate methods and design solutions that will meet preidentified needs using proper engineering standards, while paying attention to health and safety risks, environmental and societal sustainability. (Inv and Des)
3. Select and/or create, and use appropriate engineering tools, while recognizing their limitations. (Tools)
4. Ability to work effectively as a member of a team. (Teamwork)
5. Effectively communicate complex engineering concepts to different stakeholders both orally and written. (Comm)
6. Recognize their role and responsibility towards society as professional engineers, especially their primary role to protect the public. (Prof)
7. Apply economic analysis and project management to the project (Econ)
8. Identify and address their own educational need to maintain their competency in the fields necessary for the success of the project. (LL)

Note: Parenthesis refers to the GAs, which can be found at https://uwaterloo.ca/engineeri...

The Canadian Engineering Accreditation Board (CEAB) requires an engineering undergraduate curriculum to have a significant open-ended engineering design project that involves teamwork and project management. The main goal of NE408, along with NE409, is to have NE students demonstrate appropriate competencies by applying engineering design knowledge and skills gained throughout the NE undergraduate program to a substantial engineering design project that involves teamwork and project management.

FYDP FUNDING SUPPORT & OPPORTUNITIES FOR EXTERNAL (ADDITIONAL) FUNDING

Support from the NE program:

In order to help students with FYDP prototype construction expenses, the NE program provides funding support to FYDP projects at a level based on i) the merit of the NE307 proposal, and ii) the number of students in a team, up to certain maximum amounts per student and per project. 

For the 2026 NE cohort, the maximum amounts are CAD 300 per student and CAD 1200 per project.  Therefore, unless otherwise communicated to your team by the end of Week 1 of the term, a team of 4 or 5 students would receive a budget of $1200 for their FYDP, whereas a team of 3 students would receive $900.

The use of these funds is strictly limited to direct FYDP-related expenses such as materials and supplies, equipment/lab fees, etc. and requires the prior approval of the team’s consultant.

The funds are kept in designated UW accounts as available budgets and any unused amounts at the end of NE409 are returned to the NE program.

External Funding Programs:

In addition to NE program funding, students are strongly encouraged to explore and pursue external funding opportunities from industrial sponsors or FYDP-relevant funding/award programs. The faculty of engineering keeps a list of some of these programs here: https://uwaterloo.ca/capstone-design/supporters-and-financial-awards.

The following are some awards that NE FYDP teams often pursue and frequently win:

GUIDELINES FOR THE DOCUMENTS LISTED IN THE PREVIOUS SECTION:

The following are some guidelines for preparing the documents in submissions 1-4. All documents are to be written in size 12 Times New Roman font, single spaced. The length limits apply to the text parts of the documents (i.e. do not include space allocated to any figures or illustrations). The title page of a document is not included in the page count.

Design Overview & Specifications (10 pages maximum):  This document will include the following content, in separate sections, using the following subheadings:

Executive Summary (maximum 200 words): A concise articulation of the key goal of your project, the challenge it aims to address, a high-level description of your design concept (and how it addresses the challenge) and the primary specifications or customer requirements that the design needs to meet with quantitative minimum performance specification values for each.

Background (~0.5 page): A brief description of the problem the design aims to address.

o   Design Concept (~2 pages+ figures): A high-level description of your design and its various components, typically with some schematic diagrams for illustration, making connections to the relevant underlying scientific theory and/or working mechanisms. Space allocated to figures is not part of the 10 pages limit

Design Specifications:  (4 pages max + the summary table on a separate page): This section, a.k.a. Customer Requirements document, outlines the specifications or features that the design must satisfy or demonstrate in order to be deemed successful for its intended purpose. Starting with a clear definition of who your customer is (e.g. an end user or consumer, a manufacturer of a system where your product would be used as a component, a specific company, a specific UW PI, etc.), outline the specifications that the design must satisfy, providing a brief rationale/justification for each one. Note the following when preparing this section:

  • All specifications/requirements must be assigned minimum performance specifications; i.e. quantitative, measurable values (e.g.  for performance, cost, weight, yield, speed, government standards, etc.) that will allow the customer to determine if your design met their requirements.

  • The requirements/specifications are to be classified into primary (i.e. ones that must be satisfied for the design to be considered successful) and secondary (i.e. ones that are good to have for added value to the customer).  Typically, the secondary requirements are additional features or functionalities. Up to one of the secondary specifications may however just be a more stringent level of one of the primary requirements

  • The design must have at least 4 primary and 3 secondary requirements/specifications 

  • At least 3 of the primary requirements  and 2 of the secondary requirements must be functional specifications. Cost, ease of use, aesthetics, public safety and sustainability considerations are not functional specifications because they do not indicate what a design must be able “to do”. Meeting certain environmental requirements (e.g. certain biodegradability rate, emissions rate, etc.) may in some cases however be treated as functional specifications if they put certain constraints on functionality.  

  • The requirements are to be recaptured in a summary table at the end of this section. The table should show if a certain requirement is primary or secondary, ii) functional or non-functional, and  iii) its minimum performance specification.  

  • A successful design is one that meets its pre-stated primary specifications as demonstrated by the design constructed prototype.

Design Work and Trade-offs (~1.5 pages):  This section should include the following:

  • An outline of the main aspects of the design work that will be carried out to make the design satisfy the set specifications or customer requirements, making the case for the complexity and level of technical depth that the work will involve that will make it adequate for an engineering capstone project (~0.5-1 page)

  • A preliminary list of the main design trade-offs: These are the factors that you will need to reach a good balance point on in your design in your optimized design in order to meet the often conflicting demands of cost, safety, sustainability and the various min performance requirements.    Example: You are designing a slow release biodegradable fertilizer using a core shell design with a certain shell material to slow down the release of the active agents in the soil. One of the trade-offs here will be the balance between the release rate and the biodegradation rate. (~ 0.5 page)

Sustainability Considerations  (~0.5 pages):  This section should address one or both of the following points (as may be applicable):

  • If the design aims to address one (or more) of the UNSDGs https://sdgs.un.org/goals

  • Any considerations that will be given to sustainability in your design such as in the choice of materials or processes that will be utilized

Project Plan & Milestones (no page limit): The document will include:

  • The various milestones of the project plan presented in a table, showing the start and end dates of each task, as well as on a GANTT chart, taking into consideration the 408 requirements timeline

  • An identification of each group member’s areas of responsibility with respect to required tasks and the estimated number of hours for each task (per person), presented in a table

  • An outline of expected/known requirements for lab space, equipment, software, special needs.

  • A budget sheet

  • This document can be the same or a revised version of what you submitted in NE307

Verification Plan (3 pages maximum): This document will outline  the verification tools (e.g. equations or mathematical models, simulations, scientific theory, and/or empirical data) that will be used for predicting and verifying that the design can meet the functional specifications before the prototype construction starts.

  • The modelling and/or simulations can be done for individual parts of the design prototype, the final design (i.e. with all the parts of the design assembled together), or both.

  • The modelling and/or simulations can be done for one or more of the design specifications or requirements  (ideally for the main functional requirement, such as for ‘sensor sensitivity’ in case of a sensor, ‘power conversion efficiency’ in case of an energy device, etc.). To simplify things, specifications being modelled or simulated can be considered to be independent from each other.  

  • Outcomes of these models or simulations must be able to show dependencies on the design input parameters (Example: Suppose you are designing a slow-release fertilizer.  The model or simulations must be able to show that the release rate depends on the choice of granule size or shell thickness, etc.)

Important note: Conceptual verification is an essential component of engineering design and accounts for a significant portion of the NE408 grade. All FYDPs must have a component of analytical (mathematical) modelling and/or simulations that verifies that the design can meet the functional customer requirements.

Note: Design verification results (e.g. model or simulation results) are to be presented in the Design Verification Results & Analysis  later. They are not meant to be included in this document.

Test Plan (4 pages maximum): This document will outline – at a high level -  the measurements and test procedures that will be used for testing the constructed prototype in order to show that it meets the specs or customer requirements. The document should include:

  • A table presenting the Design Specifications as provided in S#1 (on page 1 of the document).

  • A testing procedure for each functional requirement that the design needs to meet

  • A quantitative value (including tolerances or statistical estimates if applicable)  of what would be considered a “passing” test result, i.e. one that indicates that a requirement or a functional specification has been met

Note: This document is not meant to include any test results. It only contains a plan of the tests that will be conducted on the constructed prototype to test if or demonstrate that the design has been successful. 

 

Design Verification Results & Analysis (no page limit): The document will

  • Outline the main aspects of your verification plan that were implemented.

  • Provide an analysis of the verification data (e.g. results of mathematical models or simulations, any supporting data from literature) to show that the design will be able to meet functional requirements. The verification data itself (e.g. simulation results) should be presented in an Appendix to the document.  The content of this subsection should primarily focus on presenting the most important results and explaining how they verify that the design will give the desired outcomes and meet the specifications.

  • Outline any deviations from the verification plan submitted earlier.

Note the following:

  • It is recognized that some design attributes or functional specifications may not lend themselves easily to simulations or mathematical modelling. In these cases a qualitative (i.e. non mathematical) analysis of your design with connections to underlying theoretical principle is acceptable.

  • Recall that verifications can be done for the individual components of your design or for the fully assembled (i.e. integrated) system (or both).

Progress Update (5 pages maximum): This document will report the progress made to-date towards the construction of the design prototype, outlining the progress that has been made in each of the following areas, each in a different section:

  • The purchase of any materials and supplies

  • Access to, and setup of, the necessary equipment

  • A description of progress made towards prototype construction

  • An account of any preliminary tests that might have been conducted to date (e.g. on some materials or certain components).  [Note: The contents of this part may vary considerably among the different FYDP groups.]

  • Issues encountered (if any), and plans to address them 

  • Acquiring any additional funds for your FYDP (if applicable)

Safety:

You must have all safety signoffs in place (with the NE lab manager) before any lab work starts.
