Course Description

Calendar Description for PHYS 115

Brief review of kinematics. Particle dynamics, work, energy, conservation of energy. Conservation of linear momentum, collisions, rotational kinematics and dynamics, conservation of angular momentum. Equilibrium of rigid bodies.

View requirements for PHYS 115

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course students should be able to:
Define and describe core concepts of classical mechanics (e.g., free fall, force, energy) and provide real-world examples to demonstrate their applications [KB, LL].
Break down complex physical scenarios into their underlying principles, identifying relationships between forces, motion, and energy; for instance, by drawing free body diagrams [KB, PA].
Identify known and unknown variables, organize given information, and formulate problem statements to solve physical problems effectively [KB, PA].
Evaluate physical problems to determine the governing mechanical principles, such as Newton's laws, conservation laws, and kinematic equations, and apply them systematically [KB, PA].
Solve for unknown quantities through algebra, calculus, and vector analysis, and validate results through unit consistency and order-of-magnitude estimates [KB, PA].
Assess the plausibility of results by considering physical constraints, assumptions, and approximations, and identify discrepancies when outcomes deviate from expectations [KB, PA, LL].
Present solutions and explanations in a structured, concise, and professional format to others (e.g., classmates, teammates, TAs, instructors), using appropriate mathematical notation, diagrams, and verbal descriptions [IaTW, LL].

Note: Abbreviations in brackets (i.e., Knowledge Base (KB), Problem Analysis (PA),  Individual and Team Work (IaTW),  Life-long Learning (LL)) are Graduate Attributes required for Accreditation by the Canadian Engineering Accreditation Board (CEAB). Missed Assessment Policy

The lowest two individual quizzes, the lowest two team quizzes, the lowest two progress reports, the lowest two assignments, and two lecture participation will be dropped—regardless of the reason. If you miss a quiz, tutorial question, or assignment, it will be recorded as a zero for grade calculation purposes. If you miss more than two assessment components, the weight of any additional missed components will be transferred to the remaining ones or the final exam under special circumstances. However, if you miss the midterm for a university-approved reason, the weighting will be transferred to the final exam. Please also read Illnesses and Absences below.

As outlined below, your final mark incorporates both in-class and out-of-class activities, individual and teamwork, as well as test-based and non-test-based assessments.

Participation in Lecture Sessions

  • Frequency: Every Lecture Session

  • Performance Metric: Individual Participation

  • Level of Collaboration Allowed: Limited – Collaboration permitted only for designated think-pair-answer questions (See the “think-pair-answer” document on LEARN).

  • Description: Each lecture session includes one or more in-class questions delivered through the Vevox platform. These may take the form of multiple-choice questions, polls, or word clouds, and are designed to assess comprehension of course content, gather feedback on course design, and evaluate instructional effectiveness. Participation in these activities also serves as an indicator of attendance, as lecture attendance is mandatory. Students must bring a personal device with secure Internet access to participate. 

  • Grading Criteria: Participation and timely submission of responses during class activities.  

  • Late Penalty: N/A – Responses must be submitted during the allotted class time. 

Bonus Marks for Course Survey

  • Frequency: 2

  • Performance Metric: Individual Performance

  • Level of Collaboration Allowed: None – Individual activity; collaboration is not permitted.

  • Description: Bonus marks are awarded for participating in two surveys about the course. The first survey happens before the midterm and the second one happens at the end of the term.

  • Grading Criteria: Full credit based on participation.

  • Late Penalty: N/A

Bonus Marks in Lecture Sessions

  • Frequency: Occasional (Ad hoc during lecture sessions)

  • Performance Metric: Individual Performance

  • Level of Collaboration Allowed: None – Individual activity; collaboration is not permitted.

  • Description: Bonus marks are awarded for answering selected multiple-choice questions during lectures. These questions assess understanding of the lecture material and may involve short calculations or error identification. Only the first ten correct responses receive bonus marks. Participation requires the use of the Vevox platform, and students must bring a personal device with secure Internet access.

  • Grading Criteria: Accuracy of response; only the first 10 correct answers are awarded marks.

  • Late Penalty: N/A – Answers must be submitted within the allotted class time.

Weekly Assignment Sets

  • Frequency: 11 Weekly Assignments

  • Performance Metric: Individual Submission

  • Level of Collaboration Allowed: Allowed – Collaboration with classmates permitted; however, each student must submit their own work.

  • Description: Weekly assignments consist of up to 20 questions and are published on WileyPLUS. They are automatically graded, and solutions are accessible upon submission. Assignments open at 7 PM on Thursdays and close at 7 PM the following Thursday.

  • Grading Criteria: Automatic grading through WileyPLUS based on accuracy of submitted answers.

  • Late Penalty: N/A – Assignment set must be submitted before the deadline.

Weekly Individual Quizzes

  • Frequency: 6 Weekly Quizzes (During Tutorials)

  • Performance Metric: Individual Submission

  • Level of Collaboration Allowed: None – Exam-like conditions.

  • Description: Weekly quizzes assess individual understanding in an exam-like setting during tutorials. Students upload their solutions to Crowdmark immediately after the session. Feedback and grades are released by 7 PM the following Friday (CHE/SEC 001 students) or Monday (MGTE/SEC 002 students). See “The 35-Minute Individual Quiz Block” section of the “tutorial structure” document on LEARN.

  • Grading Criteria: Accuracy and clarity of solutions based on the rubric detailed in – See TABLE 6 and Problem Solving Skills below.

  • Late Penalty: N/A – Quizzes must be completed and submitted during tutorial sessions.

More about the Course

Attendance and Active Participation

Attendance and active participation in lecture and tutorial sessions are essential. Active teaching and learning are implemented throughout the course, so your engagement in the course activities is of paramount importance for your learning outcomes.

Lecture and tutorial sessions begin promptly at the scheduled time slot, so it is important that you arrive on time. Your attendance is monitored through different assessment components integrated into the course.

Note: This section is adapted from a similar section in the PHYS 115 course outline, Winter 2020.

Problem Solving Skills

The goal of the exams is to test your knowledge of the material as well as your problem-solving skills. While getting help will often be necessary, you will need to maximize your problem-solving proficiency. You must build the ability to solve problems independently within a limited amount of time, without assistance. The assigned problems are the minimum of what you should master. There is a strong alignment between the quizzes, assignments, practice problems, and exams.

TAs will mark the exams; hence, showing all your work and thought processes will be necessary for partial marks. Your grade will depend on clarity, organization, and correct presentation of solutions. The weekly quizzes are designed to help you practice problem-solving and solution presentation skills. You are encouraged to think critically, creatively, and develop confidence in approaching challenging problems.

Note: This section is adapted from a similar section in the PHYS 115 course outline, Winter 2020.

Weekly Problem Sets

  • Frequency: 12 Weekly Problem Sets

  • Level of Collaboration Allowed: Allowed – Team collaboration is required during tutorial sessions and strongly encouraged outside of them.

  • Description: Weekly problem sets include up to 10 questions that mirror quiz and exam formats. Students complete at least the first two questions in teams during tutorials and submit team progress reports. Solutions for the remaining questions can be collaboratively discussed outside tutorials. Solutions for the first two questions of each problem set are published after the marks for the associated individual and team quizzes have been completed.

Student Teams

You are randomly divided into teams of three people. See the “Student Teams” documents on LEARN.

Peer-to-Peer Discussions Channel on Discord

There is a dedicated discussion channel for you on Discord. This channel focuses on improving course understanding and is intended for exchanging tips and discussing quizzes, assignments, problems, and lecture material. You are strongly encouraged to use this channel; however, you must not post or share answers to quizzes, assignments, or problems, or provide solutions for activities meant to be completed individually or in groups (Please see Administrative Policy and University Policy about academic integrity).

I frequently monitor this channel and try to address your questions as much as I can.

Generative AI

This course includes the independent development and practice of specific skills, such as problem solving and teamwork, related to the general attributes outlined by the Canadian Engineering Accreditation Board (CEAB) and used by the University of Waterloo. Therefore, the use of Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) trained using large language models (LLM) or other methods to produce text, images, music, or code, like Chat GPT, DALL-E, or GitHub CoPilot, is not permitted in this class. Unauthorized use in this course, such as running course materials through GenAI or using GenAI to complete a course assessment is considered a violation of Policy 71 (plagiarism or unauthorized aids or assistance). Work produced with the assistance of AI tools does not represent the author’s original work and is therefore in violation of the fundamental values of academic integrity including honesty, trust, respect, fairness, responsibility and courage (ICAI, n.d.). 

You should be prepared to show your work. To demonstrate your learning, you should keep your rough notes, including research notes, brainstorming, and drafting notes. You may be asked to submit these notes along with earlier drafts of their work, either through saved drafts or saved versions of a document. If the use of GenAI is suspected where not permitted, you may be asked to meet with your instructor or TA to provide explanations to support the submitted material as being your original work. Through this process, if you have not sufficiently supported your work, academic misconduct allegations may be brought to the Associate Dean. 

In addition, you should be aware that the legal/copyright status of generative AI inputs and outputs is unclear. More information is available from the Copyright Advisory Committee:  https://uwaterloo.ca/copyright... 

Students are encouraged to reach out to campus supports if they need help with their coursework including: 

  • Student Success Office for help with skills like notetaking and time management
  • Writing and Communication Centre for assignments with writing or presentations
  • AccessAbility Services for documented accommodations
  • Library for research-based assignments

University Policy

Academic integrity: In order to maintain a culture of academic integrity, members of the University of Waterloo community are expected to promote honesty, trust, fairness, respect and responsibility. [Check the Office of Academic Integrity for more information.]

Grievance: A student who believes that a decision affecting some aspect of their university life has been unfair or unreasonable may have grounds for initiating a grievance. Read Policy 70, Student Petitions and Grievances, Section 4. When in doubt, please be certain to contact the department’s administrative assistant who will provide further assistance.

Discipline: A student is expected to know what constitutes academic integrity to avoid committing an academic offence, and to take responsibility for their actions. [Check the Office of Academic Integrity for more information.] A student who is unsure whether an action constitutes an offence, or who needs help in learning how to avoid offences (e.g., plagiarism, cheating) or about “rules” for group work/collaboration should seek guidance from the course instructor, academic advisor, or the undergraduate associate dean. For information on categories of offences and types of penalties, students should refer to Policy 71, Student Discipline. For typical penalties, check Guidelines for the Assessment of Penalties.

Appeals: A decision made or penalty imposed under Policy 70, Student Petitions and Grievances (other than a petition) or Policy 71, Student Discipline may be appealed if there is a ground. A student who believes they have a ground for an appeal should refer to Policy 72, Student Appeals.

Note for students with disabilities: AccessAbility Services, located in Needles Hall, Room 1401, collaborates with all academic departments to arrange appropriate accommodations for students with disabilities without compromising the academic integrity of the curriculum. If you require academic accommodations to lessen the impact of your disability, please register with AccessAbility Services at the beginning of each academic term.

Turnitin.com: Text matching software (Turnitin®) may be used to screen assignments in this course. Turnitin® is used to verify that all materials and sources in assignments are documented. Students' submissions are stored on a U.S. server, therefore students must be given an alternative (e.g., scaffolded assignment or annotated bibliography), if they are concerned about their privacy and/or security. Students will be given due notice, in the first week of the term and/or at the time assignment details are provided, about arrangements and alternatives for the use of Turnitin in this course.

It is the responsibility of the student to notify the instructor if they, in the first week of term or at the time assignment details are provided, wish to submit alternate assignment.
