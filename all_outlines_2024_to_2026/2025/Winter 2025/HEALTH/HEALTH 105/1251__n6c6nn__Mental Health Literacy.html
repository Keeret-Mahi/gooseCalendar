Course Description

Calendar Description for HEALTH 105

This course will introduce students to mental health and wellness across the life span, with an emphasis on the post-secondary age group. HEALTH105 considers mental health within the broader spectrum of health in our changing world. Through this course, students will increase their ability to recognize, manage, and strengthen mental health. Students will develop greater awareness of their own mental health and wellness through the application of the knowledge and skills learned in this course. In addition to considering mental health at the individual level, this course will focus on how to foster a culture of support and caring in community. This course is not intended to be a substitute for counselling.

View requirements for HEALTH 105

Note that completion of this course does not qualify students to act as a mental health professional.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course students should be able to:
Describe key signs, symptoms, and treatments for common mental health conditions,
Recognize how approaches and attitudes to mental health and well-being are shaped by social determinants and intersecting personal and cultural identities,
Describe barriers and facilitators of help-seeking for mental health concerns,
Recognize empathetic ways to respond to and support others,
Critically reflect on the meaning of resilience and social ecological models of resilience,
Describe intersecting factors that contribute to stigma and how stigma affects mental health,
Develop skills and capacities that enable you to apply course concepts to your own mental well-being, and
Develop skills and capacities that enable you to apply course concepts in your community.

Important Information about Assessments

Grace Days

There are a total of 5 grace days provided to help you manage your workload during the term. Grace days are "extra" days that you have beyond the listed due to submit your assignment during which no late penalty will be applied. You can distribute your grace days to your individual assignments however you'd like. Grace days cannot be applied to the discussion board activities or the quizzes because there is less flexibility with grading these elements.

You do not need to inform your TAs or your instructor if you are planning to apply grace days. If an assignment is submitted after the deadline, then the instructional team will automatically apply the use of grace days. There is an item in the gradebook listed as “Grace Days Remaining.” This is manually updated by the instructional team as we assess assignments. It is there to let you as the student, and us as the instructional team, know how many grace days you have remaining to use for the assignments. While it is available in the gradebook (listed as a bonus item) it is an ungraded item; there are no additional bonus marks or grade benefits to using or not using the grace days – it is simply an accessible place to make the information known to you the student, and us as the instructional team. 

If an assignment is submitted after the grace days period ends, the submission is considered late, and the late penalty will apply. 

Once all grace days are used, no additional days will be provided. I encourage students to plan their grace day use for times during the semester when they know they have a variety of midterms and assignments due. 

Assignment Dropbox Information

The dropboxes in LEARN are formatted to accept multiple submissions. Therefore, if you submit your assignment, and decide that you want to make some edits or changes, you can make your changes and submit the new file. The most recent submission is the one that will be assessed for grading. 

Please also note that dropboxes within LEARN can lag when multiple submissions are occurring at the same time (which tends to happen when many submissions occur near the deadline). Therefore, if you submit your assignment at 11:53 pm but the assignment does not appear in the dropbox until 12:03 am, then the assignment is subject to grace day and / or a late penalty. Moral of the story – submit early! The same is true if you choose to make multiple submissions. If you have a copy of your assignment submitted on Sunday at 10 pm and then decide to make changes and your new submission appears on Monday at 12:01 am, it is the one that appears on the Monday at 12:01 am that will be graded and will be subject to grace day and / or late penalty application.

File Submission

There are four written assignments in this course, spaced so that they are due at weeks 3, 6, 9 and 12. This provides the instructional team adequate time to read and assess each assignment, and provide feedback to you! Assignments and associated feedback will be returned to students prior to the submission of the next assignment (i.e. feedback and grade for A1 will be provided to students before A2 submission). 

All graded assignments in HEALTH 107 are expected to be individual, authentically created work. Therefore, no sharing, collaborating, copying, or using classmates’ work (or previous cohorts’ assignments) on the writing/completion of your assignments will be tolerated. Please see the Academic Integrity section of our syllabus for more information.   

Assignments must be submitted as a MS Word document (.doc or .docx). The dropboxes are set up to only allow a submission of this file type (with the exception of A3, which will also allow .mov and .mp4 file types). If you are having trouble submitting your assignment check that it meets the file type extension requirements.

In addition to ensuring that your assignment is submitted with the correct file extension, it is also the student’s responsibility to submit the correct file to the dropbox. This is a large class, and the instructional team does not regularly monitor what is submitted. Therefore, we mark what you submit. The following are examples of potential impacts to grading if the incorrect file is submitted:

  • Submission of the blank template file will result in a mark of zero
  • Submission an assignment for a different course (e.g. submits an assignment for Psych 101 instead of Health 105) will result in a mark of zero
  • Submission a draft version, and not the ‘final good copy’ version of the assignment; the resulting mark will vary depending on the submission itself

This list of examples is not exhaustive, and resubmission of the correct file will not be permitted after the fact. Please carefully select the file that you wish to submit! I would also encourage you to double check the submission after uploading to LEARN. 

Any assignment that is submitted as a file type other than those listed (.doc, .docx, .mp4 or .mov) will not be assessed and will be awarded a mark of zero. 

Late Policy

The late penalty is 10% per day for the first five days, after which a grade of 0% will apply. The late penalty applies to the due dates of the discussion board activities and the 4 individual assignments.

Re-Grading of Assignments

We are happy to go over your graded assignment with you and provide clarification. However, students must wait 24 hours after an assignment has been returned before contacting their TA; during this time, students are encouraged to review and reflect on the feedback received. After 24 hours, students have up to 1 week (7 days) to contact their TA to ask for further feedback, clarification, or re-grading. Once beyond this deadline, additional feedback will not be provided, nor will an assignment be eligible for re-grading. The aim of this policy is to ensure that students are monitoring their course progress in a timely manner.

After reflecting on your assignment and feedback, if you still feel that your grade should be reviewed the first step is to contact your TA who marked the assignment. You will need to provide specific details and justification (e.g., Page 3, lines 7 to 12, & why you think you have earned more marks based on the rubric). After speaking with your TA, if you still have concerns, please contact your instructor directly. Please note that your entire assignment will be remarked, therefore your mark could go up, could go down, or could remain the same upon re-grading.

Short-Term Absence (STA)

The Short-Term Absence is an absence of 2 calendar days or less during the formal lecture period. The absence can be for any reason that prevents a student from meeting their academic obligations. No accompanying documentation is requested or required. Only one short-term absence can be self-declared per academic term, and it applies to all of the student’s courses. During the two-day academic consideration period, instructors cannot require completion of any academic responsibilities. However, per University regulations, there is no expectation that a self-declared short-term absence be total; that is, students may elect to participate in any course and complete any course element despite not being required to. These absences must be declared through Quest and students must connect with all course instructors directly to discuss any missed or delayed tests or assignments. Students must contact the instructor no later than 24 hours after the missed assessment(s). If possible, students should contact the instructor prior to the expected missed assessment(s). Please note that the course/class components of Laboratory (LAB) and Clinic (CLN) do not qualify for accommodation due to a self-declared short-term absence. 

COVID-19 / Cold / Flu Related Absence Declaration

Is an absence of 10 days or less due to COVID-19 / cold / flu-related illness (including vaccination side-effects) or a requirement to self-isolate. Participation in online course elements may be reasonable. Students can self-declare one COVID-19 / cold / flu-related absence in an academic term, and it applies to all of the student’s courses. These absences must be declared through Quest and students must connect with all course instructors directly to discuss any missed or delayed tests or assignments within 24-hours of the missed assessment(s) (where reasonable). Absences of more than 10 days must be supported using a Verification of Illness Form declaration.

Verification of Illness Form (VIF) Declaration 

A VIF is documentation to support a declaration of absence. It is for absences that do not meet the short-term absence criteria (or when a student has used their self-declared short-term absence previously during the term), COVID-19 absence criteria, or religious accommodation criteria, and may also be used for retroactive reports of illness. Students must complete the Verification of Illness or Extenuating Circumstances web form (log-in required) and upload their Verification of Illness Form (VIF) and/or other supporting documentation to the site. If the University’s VIF is not provided, the documentation should include the same information as the VIF, including degree of incapacitation (e.g., severe, moderate, slight, or negligible) and associated dates. Students must connect with all course instructors directly to discuss any missed or delayed tests or assignments within 24-hours of the missed assessment(s) (where reasonable). The webform includes a list of courses students are currently enrolled in, and students are required to indicate any missed course elements. Students should not be submitting their Verification of Illness Form (or other documentation) directly to instructors or advisors. The UG Associate Dean’s Office will review each submission, including documentation, and determine whether to approve the absence request. Once approved, students and instructors will receive a confirmation email of the absence. Advisors will be notified if there are questions or concerns about a student’s well-being, such as submitting multiple VIFs in one academic term or documentation for a lengthy absence.

Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI)

This course includes the independent development and practice of specific skills. Therefore, the use of Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) trained using large language models (LLM) or other methods to produce text, images, music, or code, like Chat GPT, DALL-E, or GitHub CoPilot, is not permitted in this class. Unauthorized use in this course, such as running course materials through GenAI or using GenAI to complete a course assessment is considered a violation of Policy 71 (plagiarism or unauthorized aids or assistance). Work produced with the assistance of AI tools does not represent the author’s original work and is therefore in violation of the fundamental values of academic integrity including honesty, trust, respect, fairness, responsibility and courage (ICAI, n.d.). 

You should be prepared to show your work. To demonstrate your learning, you should keep your rough notes, including research notes, brainstorming, and drafting notes. You may be asked to submit these notes along with earlier drafts of their work, either through saved drafts or saved versions of a document. If the use of GenAI is suspected where not permitted, you may be asked to meet with your instructor or TA to provide explanations to support the submitted material as being your original work. Through this process, if you have not sufficiently supported your work, academic misconduct allegations may be brought to the Associate Dean. 

In addition, you should be aware that the legal/copyright status of generative AI inputs and outputs is unclear. More information is available from the Copyright Advisory Committee:  https://uwaterloo.ca/copyright...

Intellectual Property Copyright Violations

Academic integrity is a cornerstone of any educational institution. Students and faculty alike benefit when academic integrity is well understood, encouraged and maintained. The University and faculty strongly discourage students from participating in/uploading course materials on online sharing platforms, such as Course Hero, OneClass and similar platforms. In many cases, the material in question is created and owned by the faculty member or instructor. Faculty are indeed presumed to be the Intellectual Property Owners of the course materials. 

Students are strongly advised to err on the side of caution and ask for permission from their instructors before sharing and/or distributing any course materials. These course materials will usually be made available only for personal use by students. Students may not distribute or reproduce the materials, whether for commercial purposes or not, without the express written consent of the faculty or instructor. Neither faculty nor the instructors waive their intellectual property rights when granting limited use of their course materials to students. 

Uploading course materials, including lectures, quizzes, and exams, onto online sharing platforms such as Course Hero, OneClass, Facebook, Reddit, and similar platforms is considered to be a copyright violation, regardless of whether an individual did so intentionally or unintentionally. Ignorance of the law is not an excuse.

Students should be aware that this course contains the intellectual property of their instructor, TA, and/or the University of Waterloo. Intellectual property includes items such as: 

  • Lecture content, spoken and written (and any audio/video recording thereof);
  • Lecture handouts, presentations, and other materials prepared for the course (e.g., PowerPoint slides);
  • Questions or solution sets from various types of assessments (e.g., assignments, quizzes, tests, final exams); and
  • Work protected by copyright (e.g., any work authored by the instructor or TA or used by the instructor or TA with permission of the copyright owner).

Course materials and the intellectual property contained therein, are used to enhance a student’s educational experience. However, sharing this intellectual property without the intellectual property owner’s permission is a violation of intellectual property rights. For this reason, it is necessary to ask the instructor, TA and/or the University of Waterloo for permission before uploading and sharing the intellectual property of others online (e.g., to an online repository). Permission from an instructor, TA or the University is also necessary before sharing the intellectual property of others from completed courses with students taking the same/similar courses in subsequent terms/years. In many cases, instructors might be happy to allow distribution of certain materials. However, doing so without expressed written consent is considered a violation of intellectual property rights. Please alert the instructor if you become aware of intellectual property belonging to others (past or present) circulating, either through the student body or online. The intellectual property rights owner deserves to know (and may have already given their consent).

Counselling Services - Additional Details 

Counselling Services (Needles Hall, 2nd Floor) is a team of professionals that provides programming and services to help students lead a healthy and balanced life. They strive to provide a secure, supportive environment for students of all orientations and backgrounds. Counselling services are provided, at no charge, in a private and confidential setting to University of Waterloo students who are currently enrolled in classes or on a co-op term. 

  • Booked Appointments – Call 519-888-4096 to book an appointment.
    • Monday to Friday: 8:30 a.m.- 4:30 p.m.
    • Monday to Thursday: evening appointment available from 5:00 p.m.- 7:00 p.m.

In addition, Counselling Services offers wellness programs and student groups, self-directed programs, and pre-recorded workshops and seminars. See this page for more details on resources that they offer. 

If students are out of the province, they should seek care where they are located or contact Empower Me. Empower Me is a confidential mental health and wellness service available 24/7, 365 days a year. To access Empower Me, students need to create an account on the Dialogue mobile app or on the web at www.studentcare.ca/dialogue. To be covered by Empower Me a student must be enrolled in the WUSA or GSPA Health Plan, administered by Studentcare.

University Policy

Academic integrity: In order to maintain a culture of academic integrity, members of the University of Waterloo community are expected to promote honesty, trust, fairness, respect and responsibility. [Check the Office of Academic Integrity for more information.]

Grievance: A student who believes that a decision affecting some aspect of their university life has been unfair or unreasonable may have grounds for initiating a grievance. Read Policy 70, Student Petitions and Grievances, Section 4. When in doubt, please be certain to contact the department’s administrative assistant who will provide further assistance.

Discipline: A student is expected to know what constitutes academic integrity to avoid committing an academic offence, and to take responsibility for their actions. [Check the Office of Academic Integrity for more information.] A student who is unsure whether an action constitutes an offence, or who needs help in learning how to avoid offences (e.g., plagiarism, cheating) or about “rules” for group work/collaboration should seek guidance from the course instructor, academic advisor, or the undergraduate associate dean. For information on categories of offences and types of penalties, students should refer to Policy 71, Student Discipline. For typical penalties, check Guidelines for the Assessment of Penalties.

Appeals: A decision made or penalty imposed under Policy 70, Student Petitions and Grievances (other than a petition) or Policy 71, Student Discipline may be appealed if there is a ground. A student who believes they have a ground for an appeal should refer to Policy 72, Student Appeals.

Note for students with disabilities: AccessAbility Services, located in Needles Hall, Room 1401, collaborates with all academic departments to arrange appropriate accommodations for students with disabilities without compromising the academic integrity of the curriculum. If you require academic accommodations to lessen the impact of your disability, please register with AccessAbility Services at the beginning of each academic term.

Turnitin.com: Text matching software (Turnitin®) may be used to screen assignments in this course. Turnitin® is used to verify that all materials and sources in assignments are documented. Students' submissions are stored on a U.S. server, therefore students must be given an alternative (e.g., scaffolded assignment or annotated bibliography), if they are concerned about their privacy and/or security. Students will be given due notice, in the first week of the term and/or at the time assignment details are provided, about arrangements and alternatives for the use of Turnitin in this course.

It is the responsibility of the student to notify the instructor if they, in the first week of term or at the time assignment details are provided, wish to submit alternate assignment.
