Course Description

Calendar Description for SFM 310

This course examines financial market tools, processes, and institutions which influence the environmental and the channels by which they can be utilized to promote sustainable and socially responsible long-term growth.

View requirements for SFM 310

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course students should be able to...
1. Understand the concept of sustainable finance and its significance in the financial industry. *A, G
2. Analyze the financial risks associated with climate change and their potential impacts in terms of the stability of the financial system. *A, G
3. Describe the opportunities and challenges that sustainable finance brings to the financial sector. *A, B, D, G
4. Connect issues and concepts discussed in this course to their roles in sustainability and financial markets.*A, C, G
5. Discuss the sustainability practices banks and financial market participants engage in and how those practices have changed over time. *A, C, D, G

Each of the School of Accounting and Finance’s Program Level learning outcomes identifies a knowledge, skill or value of a financial professional. These outcomes are organized into seven areas . They reflect the integration of all areas. All outcomes are also developed through experiential learning.

This course’s learning outcomes map to the Program Level learning outcomes as follows: *(A) Knowledge Base for a Financial Professional, (B) Communication Capabilities, (C) Problem-Solving Capabilities, (D) Fluency in the Languages of Business, Entrepreneurship and Technology, (E) Ethical Conduct and Social Responsibility, (F) Leadership and Collaboration, (G) Attributes / Qualities of a Financial Professional

Group Assignments:

All members of a team normally receive the team’s grade for each assignment. Should any significant teamwork issues arise, see your instructor as soon as the issue occurs, not after the assignment has been evaluated.

Objective: To promote effective teamwork and collaboration, all assignments involving group work should be completed by groups of at least 2 people.

Rationale: Collaborative efforts are vital for fostering diverse perspectives, leveraging collective knowledge, and enhancing problem-solving capabilities. Engaging in group work facilitates the exchange of ideas and strengthens interpersonal skills, preparing students for real-world team environments.

Benefits of Group Work:

o Diverse Perspectives: Multiple viewpoints lead to comprehensive analysis and innovative solutions.

o Shared Responsibilities: Tasks are distributed, ensuring a balanced workload for each team member.

o Enhanced Learning: Interaction and discussions deepen understanding and knowledge retention.

o Peer Support: Supportive environment fosters learning from each other's strengths and experiences.

General Guidelines for group assignments:

1. Group Size: Create groups of at least 2 individuals for all group assignments.

2. Collaboration: Foster an environment of open communication, respect, and active participation.

3. Shared Effort: Distribute responsibilities equitably to ensure fair contributions from all team members.

4. Deadlines: Plan and adhere to realistic timelines for successful project completion.

5. Conflict Resolution: Address any conflicts constructively and seek assistance from the instructor if necessary.

6. Peer Evaluation: Evaluate individual contributions to ensure fairness and accountability.

7. Remember, successful group work leads to enriched learning experiences and prepares you for future collaborative endeavors.

Written requests for re-grading a mid-term examination must normally be made within one week after the examination has been returned. A written request must be submitted that indicates your reasons for believing that a question was improperly graded. The instructor reserves the right to re-grade the entire examination.

You are accountable for the content and accuracy of all work you submit in this class, including any supported by generative AI.

Administrative Policy

SCHOOL OF ACCOUNTING & FINANCE POLICIES:

School of Accounting and Finance Academic Integrity Expectations:

Students at Waterloo and in SAF are expected to act with integrity, even when faced with difficult situations. Information on basic university expectations are provided below in “University Policies”. In addition, integrity is the cornerstone of the accounting and finance professions. What you practice at the University of Waterloo and in SAF will inform and even define who you are as an A&F professional. As a student at the University of Waterloo and in SAF you are expected to know what constitutes academic integrity, avoid committing offences and take responsibility for your actions. While there are common university wide expectations, you will also be expected to know and comply with course specific expectations as well. Participating in the course introduction and reviewing the course syllabus are basic expectations and are helpful regarding course specific academic requirements.

Policy 71 governs student discipline on campus and SAF works with the Associate Deans of a student’s home faculty when reporting academic misconduct. All suspected academic misconduct, regardless of type or severity, is reported to the appropriate Associate Dean who will review the evidence and apply the appropriate discipline and penalties.

Specific considerations for SAF students:

  • Students majoring in accounting and finance programs at UWaterloo should be aware that, due to the highly structured nature of the study plans and the fact that many AFM courses are offered on a limited basis, a penalty imposed because of an academic offence could result in a significant delay of the student’s degree completion and convocation dates - particularly if the penalty involves a suspension.

  • Students placed on disciplinary probation will not be granted access to activities where they are viewed as an SAF representative. For example, students represent SAF in the AFM Fellowship Program, the Student Investment or Venture Fund, at case competitions, as TAs or Research Assistants, and as recruitment ambassadors. This is not an exhaustive list. When applying to participate in these activities, students are expected to declare if they are/are not on disciplinary probation. Normally, this access is denied for a period that spans two complete academic terms subsequent to the term in which the offence occurs.

The academic integrity expectations for each of the assessments in this course have been outlined above as have the potentially unique implications for SAF students who fail to meet integrity expectations. These expectations are provided for further clarity and accompany the expectations outlined below in the discipline section (university policies) and do not replace or reduce the importance of these expectations. If ever you are unsure about what’s expected of you, you are responsible for seeking clarification from an appropriate source. “I did not know” is not an acceptable personal justification or formal defense to an allegation.

Accommodations for Missed Assessments:

Students are expected to complete all course assessments and write examinations as scheduled; however, there may be circumstances where accommodating a missed assessment is approved. See your course syllabus for specific accommodation policies that apply to your course. If an accommodation for a missed course assessment is approved, the instructor will determine the appropriate accommodation. If an accommodation is approved regarding a missed final exam, you need to consult with the course instructor about writing a deferred final exam. It is important to understand that, particularly with a cooperative education program, it is possible a deferred writing date will not be perfectly convenient to the student. If necessary, a CEE representative may be able to assist a student in their communications with an employer, however, it is still the student’s responsibility to initiate and manage any such process. Other than in exceptional circumstances, students are expected to prioritize writing the exam and completing the course (including making necessary arrangements for time away from work and being on campus). At the end of the current term, you will receive an INC course grade. When you have written the final exam, a grade revision will be submitted based on the results of the final exam and your other course work. Failure to write the final exam at the next SAF identified opportunity will result in a course grade based on the elements of the course that you completed. UW’s policy regarding requests for accommodation can be found at http://ugradcalendar.uwaterloo.ca/page/Acad-Regs-Accommodations.

SAF Process for Requesting Accommodation for Missed Assessments:

UW’s policy regarding requests for accommodation can be found at http://ugradcalendar.uwaterloo.ca/page/Acad-Regs-Accommodations. To be considered for an academic accommodation, SAF students must follow the Arts faculty process: https://uwaterloo.ca/arts/undergraduate/student-support/accommodations-illness-or-extenuating-circumstances. If you do not utilize one of the indicated approved methods that can justify missing an assessment (and receiving some sort of accommodation) you will receive 0 for that course element missed. Accordingly, it is important to understand and properly utilize the appropriate process and indicating that someone did not know of a process will not be an accepted justification. If an accommodation for a missed assessment is processed with the Faculty of Arts, students connect with the instructor to see if an accommodation can be offered. If an accommodation is processed for a missed final exam, the University of Waterloo INC grade process will be followed, where all course requirements are not completed during the term.

Changes to in-class Meeting Schedule and other Covid related Circumstances:

It is possible, due to Covid-19 or some other significant ongoing event, that the university may need to cancel classes for a short period of time and/or transition in person instruction to an online format during the term (and with short notice). In the event of a university required change, or even short-term instructor unavailability due to Covid, using an appropriate technology platform we will normally adopt an alternative meeting schedule that, as much as possible, mirrors/matches the existing in-person meeting schedule for the course/section. Both lectures and exams can be accessed remotely via technology platforms such as Teams, WebEx, Zoom, or MyLab.

Note that for students Covid-specific accommodations need to be registered through the process mentioned above. Consult the Accommodations for Missed Assessments as well as SAF Process for Requesting Accommodation for Missed Assessments sections. Any on-campus in-person assessments that require modification due to a university mandated change connected to in-person gathering will, if necessary, proceed in a manner that best achieves the intentions of the original assessment format. Any necessary changes will be communicated as soon as we reasonably can during the course of the term.

Textbooks and Intellectual Property Rights:

The content in textbooks and other course materials is subject to copyright protection, regardless of the format (i.e. hardcopy, binder version or e-text). Printing a copy of a text or creating a pdf copy of a text may infringe the legal rights of the copyright owner and could result in penalties determined in a court of law. Utilizing an e-version of a text that has been originally procured from another jurisdiction can also amount to infringing use. It is important that as a user of copyright material you follow University guidelines for copying for situations where copying is acceptable (see https://uwaterloo.ca/copyright-guidelines/). For further details, refer to the preamble of Policy 73 Intellectual Property Rights for affirmation of the University’s recognition of intellectual property rights. Consult your professor if you have any questions in this regard.

Academic Offenses and Implications:

Students majoring in accounting and finance programs at UWaterloo should be aware that, due to the highly structured nature of the study plans and the fact that many AFM courses are offered on a limited basis, a penalty imposed as a result of an academic offence could result in a significant delay of the student’s degree completion and convocation dates - particularly if the penalty involves a suspension.

Students placed on disciplinary probation will not be granted access to activities where they are viewed as a SAF representative. For example, students represent SAF in the AFM Fellowship Program, the Student Investment or Venture Fund, at case competitions, as TAs or Research Assistants, as Peer Support Learning Champions and recruitment ambassadors. This is not an exhaustive list. When applying to participate in these activities, students are expected to declare if they are/are not on disciplinary probation. If on probation, access to activities is normally denied for a period that spans two complete academic terms subsequent to the term in which the offence occurs.

University Policy

Mental Health: At the University of Waterloo, we are dedicated to supporting your mental and emotional well-being. Our Counselling Services offer confidential support, including individual counselling, workshops, and crisis intervention.
If you're struggling, please reach out for help at 519-888-4096 or visit their website for more information.

Academic integrity: In order to maintain a culture of academic integrity, members of the University of Waterloo community are expected to promote honesty, trust, fairness, respect and responsibility. [Check the Office of Academic Integrity for more information.]

Grievance: A student who believes that a decision affecting some aspect of their university life has been unfair or unreasonable may have grounds for initiating a grievance. Read Policy 70, Student Petitions and Grievances, Section 4. When in doubt, please be certain to contact the department’s administrative assistant who will provide further assistance.

Discipline: A student is expected to know what constitutes academic integrity to avoid committing an academic offence, and to take responsibility for their actions. [Check the Office of Academic Integrity for more information.] A student who is unsure whether an action constitutes an offence, or who needs help in learning how to avoid offences (e.g., plagiarism, cheating) or about “rules” for group work/collaboration should seek guidance from the course instructor, academic advisor, or the undergraduate associate dean. For information on categories of offences and types of penalties, students should refer to Policy 71, Student Discipline. For typical penalties, check Guidelines for the Assessment of Penalties.

Appeals: A decision made or penalty imposed under Policy 70, Student Petitions and Grievances (other than a petition) or Policy 71, Student Discipline may be appealed if there is a ground. A student who believes they have a ground for an appeal should refer to Policy 72, Student Appeals.

Note for students with disabilities and disabling conditions: The University of Waterloo recognizes its obligations under the Ontario Human Rights Code to accommodate students with known or suspected disabilities and disabling conditions (e.g. medical conditions, injuries, impacts of trauma such as from violence or discrimination) to the point of undue hardship. To support this obligation, AccessAbility Services (AAS) collaborates with all academic departments and schools to facilitate academic accommodations for students with disabilities and disabling conditions without compromising the academic integrity of the curriculum. If you believe you may require academic accommodations (e.g., testing accommodations, classroom accommodations), register with AAS as early in the term as possible by completing the online application. Students already registered with AAS must activate their accommodations for each of their courses at the beginning of each term using AAS' online system. If you require assistance, contact AAS by phone (519-888-4567 ext. 35082), email (access@uwaterloo.ca) or in-person (Needles Hall North, 1st Floor, Room 1401). 

Turnitin.com: Text matching software (Turnitin®) may be used to screen assignments in this course. Turnitin® is used to verify that all materials and sources in assignments are documented. Students' submissions are stored on a U.S. server, therefore students must be given an alternative (e.g., scaffolded assignment or annotated bibliography), if they are concerned about their privacy and/or security. Students will be given due notice, in the first week of the term and/or at the time assignment details are provided, about arrangements and alternatives for the use of Turnitin in this course.

It is the responsibility of the student to notify the instructor if they, in the first week of term or at the time assignment details are provided, wish to submit alternate assignment.
